# Interview Prep

Generated by `job-search-setup`. Three parts: a **prep pack** (research written to a note), a **mock interview** (in chat), and a **debrief** after the real interview. Default mock style: **{{MOCK_STYLE}}**. Interview language: {{LANGUAGE}}.

Facts about {{USER}} come only from the profile, the story bank, and what {{USER}} says. Never invent numbers, outcomes or experience, even in a "model answer".

## Step 1: Gather the inputs

Ask only for what you can't find:
- **Company and role**, and the **job description** (link or pasted text). If there's no link, look for the posting in `{{DATA}}/Job-Watch.md` and the tracker.
- **Stage:** recruiter screen, hiring manager, technical / case / presentation, panel, final / executive.
- **Interviewers** (names and titles) and the **date**, if known. If a calendar connector is available, look for the event and take attendees from it.
- Anything the recruiter told {{USER}} about the format.

Then read in parallel:
- `{{DATA}}/Profile.md` and `{{DATA}}/Stories.md`.
- `{{DATA}}/Companies/<Company>.md`, if `{{FIT}}` saved a brief.
- `{{DATA}}/Interviews/<Company>/`: earlier rounds. What was asked, what went well, open concerns. Never repeat prep that's already done; build on it.
- The tracker row: {{TRACKER_READ}}

## Step 2: Research

Use WebSearch (several in one turn) and WebFetch:
- **The job description:** must-haves vs nice-to-haves, the problems the role exists to solve, the words they repeat. Ashby pages need `https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/<org>?includeCompensation=true`.
- **Culture and values:** the company's careers, culture, values or "how we work" pages, leadership principles, engineering or company blog. Note the exact wording of their values; interviewers often score against them.
- **The company now:** product, customers, competitors, last ~12 months of news, strategy statements from leadership.
- **The interview process:** Glassdoor, Blind, Reddit or similar reports for this company and role family: stages, question styles, take-homes. These are anecdotal; say so.
- **The interviewers:** public professional background only (role, tenure, past companies, talks or posts). Useful for tailoring questions to ask. Nothing personal.

## Step 4: Mock interview

Offer three formats (AskUserQuestion):
- **Full mock:** 6–10 questions in the order a real interviewer at this stage would ask, ~30–45 minutes.
- **Rapid fire:** 10–15 short questions, quick feedback, for warm-up.
- **Focus:** only the weak spots: gaps, the hardest likely questions, or a theme {{USER}} names.

Then:
1. **Set the scene** in one line: who you're playing (title, style, drawn from the real interviewer if known), the format, and how to answer (type, or paste a voice transcript; "skip" and "pause" work).
2. **One question at a time.** Wait for the answer. Stay in character.
3. **Follow up like a real interviewer** when an answer is vague: "What did *you* do there?", "What was the number?", "What would you do differently?", "How did you know it worked?". At most two follow-ups per question.
4. **Feedback.** In `coaching` style, after each answer; in `realistic` style, all at the end. {{USER}} can switch at any time. For each answer:
   - Score 1–5 on **structure** (clear STAR or point-first), **specificity** (numbers, own actions), **relevance** (answers the question, ties to the role), **concision**.
   - What landed, what was missing, in two or three bullets.
   - A tighter outline using only facts {{USER}} gave; where a fact is missing, write `<the number>` and ask for it.
5. **Scorecard** at the end: scores per question, the three biggest fixes, the stories that worked, and questions to practise again. Would this interviewer move {{USER}} to the next round? Give an honest yes / borderline / no with a reason.

## Step 5: Save and improve

- Append a `## Mock <date>` section to the prep pack with the scorecard and the questions asked (not the full transcript).
- **Story bank:** when {{USER}} told a good story that isn't in `{{DATA}}/Stories.md`, or improved one, propose the new or updated STAR entry and add it with their OK. Note in **Used in** where it was used.
- Offer another round on the weakest areas.

## Debrief mode (after the real interview)

When {{USER}} says how an interview went:
1. Ask what was asked, what went well, what felt weak, what the interviewers emphasised, and next steps. Accept a messy brain-dump.
2. Append `## Debrief <date>` to the prep pack: questions asked, signals (positive and negative), concerns to address next round, next step and date.
3. Update the story bank's **Used in** lines.
4. Hand over to `{{TRACKER}}` (Mode 2) to update Stage and Notes, or offer to do it now.
5. Offer a short thank-you note as a draft, specific to something discussed. Never send it.
6. If there's a next round, note what to prepare differently.

## Tips & Gotchas

- The prep pack is for {{USER}} to skim in 10 minutes before the call. Keep it tight; put depth in the mock, not the note.
- Values pages are often vague; the job description's repeated words and the interview reports say more about what's actually scored.
- If {{USER}} is short on time, skip straight to the top 5 questions and a focus mock.
- Don't present Glassdoor or Reddit reports as the company's real process; they're one person's experience.
